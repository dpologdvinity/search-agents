    HOW IT DECIDES
    YOUR BEST REPLY
    For each move it considers, it assumes you answer with the reply that hurts it most, then picks the move whose worst case is best.
    PRUNING
    As soon as one of your replies shows a move is worse than an option it already has, it stops examining that move. Same answer as plain minimax, a fraction of the work.
    SCORING
    At the edge of its search it counts material (kings worth more), how far men have advanced, the home row, and the centre.

    HOW IT WORKS

    The checkers agents look ahead through the game tree and score the positions at the end of a line with a hand-written evaluation, or, for Monte Carlo tree search, with the results of random games. The page offers five agents on three board sizes, with forced or optional captures. You can play any agent, or watch two of them play each other, each with its own strength.

    Alpha-beta search

    New to this? While planning, once you find a line that is already worse than an option you have, stop looking at that line. You never needed to know how bad it was, so the same planning finishes sooner.

    Negamax with alpha-beta pruning, searched one ply deeper each round (iterative deepening) until the time budget runs out. The best move from the deepest finished round is played. Each round tries the previous best move first, and a transposition table reuses results for positions reached by different move orders.

    • Time budget 0.2 s, 0.8 s or 2.0 s at Fast, Normal and Strong, on every board size
    • When a capture is pending, the search keeps going past its depth limit until the captures run out

    Plain minimax

    New to this? Plan a fixed number of moves for both sides, assume each side makes its best reply, score the position at the end, and pick the first move that leads to the best result. It is simple, but every extra move of planning multiplies the work.

    Negamax with no pruning, searching to a fixed depth. At the same depth, alpha-beta returns the same score with less work.

    • Depth depends on the board and the capture rule: 2 to 5 plies with forced captures on 8x8, fewer on bigger boards
    • Fixed depth because it has no time limit: without pruning, each extra ply multiplies the work by the number of moves, and optional captures add many more

    Monte Carlo tree search

    New to this? Instead of scoring every line, it plays many quick random games from the position and keeps track of which first moves win the most of them. It spends more games on the moves that look best, and it needs no hand-written evaluation to decide between them.

    UCT: each iteration walks down the tree by the upper confidence bound (average result plus a bonus for moves tried rarely), adds one new position, plays a rollout, and passes the result back up. Rollouts stop after 40 plies and are scored by the same evaluation as the tree searches. They prefer captures when one exists. The seed is fixed for each position, so the same position always gets the same answer.

    • Iterations per move: 300, 1,000 or 2,000 on 8x8 at Fast, Normal and Strong; fewer on the bigger boards
    • Reports a win rate for each move, not a score

    Greedy and random

    New to this? Greedy takes the move that looks best right after it, without thinking about your reply. Random picks any legal move. They are the baselines: a search agent should beat both, and greedy shows what a one-move look ahead is worth.

    Greedy scores each legal move by the evaluation after it, and scores a move that leaves you no legal reply as a win. Random chooses uniformly from the legal moves, from a generator with a fixed seed.

    • Neither changes with the strength setting
    • Greedy is deterministic; random repeats its choices for the same position

    Rules and board sizes

    Every board uses the same American rules. Men move and capture forward only, kings move and capture one step in any direction (no flying kings), a man that reaches the far row is crowned and its move ends there, and a side with no legal move loses. Boards are 8×8 (12 men each), 10×10 (20 men each) and 12×12 (30 men each); a game is drawn after 80 plies with no capture and no man moving.

    Forced or optional captures. With forced captures (the default, standard rules) you must capture when you can. With optional captures you may make a simple move even when a capture is available. Either way, a capture chain is played to its end: once a piece starts jumping, it must keep jumping while it can, so no move stops partway through a chain. Where a player declines to capture, the search scores the position as it stands rather than in the middle of a capture.

    Chance (fixed odds)

    The chance opponent does no search and no reasoning, like the 2048 tile spawner, which places a 2 with 90% and a 4 with 10%. Each legal move gets a fixed weight by its type. The weights are renormalised over the legal moves and one move is sampled. It is seeded from the position, so the same position always gets the same move.

    Move typeExampleWeight
    CaptureAny move that jumps a piece (a chain counts once).5
    Man into the centreA man that moves onto one of the central 4×4 squares.3
    Other man moveAny other step by a man.2

    Heuristics and their values

    HeuristicWhat it measuresValue / weight
    ManA regular piece on the board.100 points
    AdvancementHow far a man has moved toward the crowning row.+3 per row: 0 on its home row, 3 × (rows − 1) on the crowning row (21 on 8x8)
    Home-row guardA man still on its own back row, which keeps the opponent from crowning there.+8
    KingA crowned piece. No position term.165 points
    CentreA man or king on one of the eight central squares, the middle 4×4 block of the board.+6
    WinTerminal position for either side.100,000

    How these were chosen: hand-set. The constants are fixed in checkers/search.py, and no tuning run or results file is committed for them. The evaluation has no mobility term. The score is always from the side to move's point of view, with each side's pieces summed. The same weights apply on every board size; the advancement and centre terms are measured on the board's own rows and columns.
