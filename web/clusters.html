HOW IT DECIDES
K-MEANS
Each point joins its nearest centroid, then each centroid moves to the mean of its points. The shaded regions are the Voronoi cells of the centroids.
DBSCAN
Points with at least minPts neighbours inside eps are core points. Cores connect into clusters, and everything unreached is noise. Hover to see a point's eps circle.
EM
Each cluster is an ellipse. The E step shares every point among the ellipses, the M step refits them. Colours blend by how much each ellipse claims a point.

HOW IT WORKS

Three ways to split points into groups. k-means cuts space into k regions and alternates two steps until nothing moves. DBSCAN grows clusters through dense regions and labels what is left as noise. A Gaussian mixture fits k ellipses with EM, giving every point a soft probability for each. All three run in your browser, step by step, from the same seed as the Python package (clusters/).

k-means (Lloyd's algorithm)

New to this? Put k markers on the map at random spots. Every point hands itself to the nearest marker. Then each marker moves to the middle of the points it holds. Repeat until no point changes hands.

One iteration is two steps. Assign: each point takes the nearest centroid, by squared Euclidean distance. Update: each centroid becomes the mean of its points. The inertia (within-cluster sum of squares) never increases across steps, and there are only finitely many labellings, so the run stops.

Seeding matters. k-means++ picks the first centroid at random, then draws each next one with probability proportional to its squared distance from the nearest centroid already chosen, so the seeds spread out. Random seeding can drop two centroids into one blob and leave another blob with none.

DBSCAN (density clustering)

New to this? Draw a circle of radius eps around each point. If the circle holds enough points, the spot is crowded and counts as a core. Crowded spots that touch form one cluster; lonely points that touch no crowd are noise.

A point is a core when at least minPts points, itself included, lie within eps. Cores within eps of each other join one cluster, and the cluster grows like a flood fill through the cores. A border point is not core but sits within eps of one; it joins its lowest-index core neighbour's cluster. The rest is noise. The number of clusters is not chosen: it falls out of eps and minPts.

Gaussian mixture by EM

New to this? Each cluster is a soft blob: a centre and a stretched, tilted ellipse. Every point is partly owned by each blob. EM alternates: first ask how much each blob owns each point, then move every blob to fit the points it owns, and repeat.

E step: responsibility rik = πk N(xi | μk, Σk) / Σj πj N(xi | μj, Σj). M step: Nk = Σi rik, μk = Σ rik xi / Nk, Σk = Σ rik (xi − μk)(xi − μk)ᵀ / Nk, πk = Nk / n. The average log-likelihood never decreases, and the run stops when it gains less than 1e-6.

The elbow

New to this? More clusters always fit better. The elbow chart shows how much each extra cluster helps. Where the drops flatten out is a fair guess at the right k.

The lab runs k-means for k = 1 to 10 with the same seed and plots the final inertia. Silhouette is the complementary check: for each point, how close it sits to its own cluster compared with the nearest other cluster, averaged over points, from −1 to 1. DBSCAN noise is left out of the silhouette.

Parameters and defaults

ParameterDefaultWhy this value
k (k-means, EM)4k is the one number you must know in advance. The presets have 4 blobs, 3 stretched blobs, 2 rings, 2 moons and 4 smiley parts (so 4 fits most of them); the elbow chart shows what the data supports.
Initialisationk-means++Spreads the starting centres apart, so runs land in one good solution far more often than random starts. Random starts are kept to show the failure.
Max iterations (k-means)100Lloyd's algorithm converges in a few steps on these sizes; the cap is a safety net, and the readout says if it was hit.
eps (DBSCAN)0.08On the unit square, 0.08 links the points of one blob and of one ring into a single cluster. Too small splits clusters into arcs; too large merges neighbours.
minPts (DBSCAN)5Counts the point itself. Five is enough to rule out a pair of stray points forming a cluster, and low enough that thin rings keep their core points.
EM tolerance1e-6Stop when the average log-likelihood gains less than this in one iteration. Below that, the fitted ellipses no longer visibly move.
EM max iterations200EM converges slowly when clusters overlap; the cap bounds the animation, and the readout says if it was hit.
EM covariance regulariser1e-6Added to the covariance diagonal so a component fitted to a few points cannot collapse to zero width.

Strengths and weaknesses

MethodStrengthsWeaknesses
k-meansFast (each step is one pass over the points); simple; the elbow tells you where k should be.Needs k up front. Assumes round clusters of similar size: on rings and moons it cuts the shapes in half. Sensitive to the start.
DBSCANFinds clusters of any shape, including rings and moons, and marks noise. No k needed.Struggles when clusters have very different densities: one eps cannot fit both. Sensitive to eps; the page's eps circles show why.
EM (Gaussian mixture)Soft labels, and each cluster's shape and tilt. Fits the stretched blobs that k-means cuts wrongly.Needs k. Assumes each cluster is Gaussian, so rings and moons fit badly. Can converge to a local optimum.

Reference implementation: the Python package clusters/ runs the same algorithms with the same seeds (python -m clusters run --algo kmeans --k 4 --data blobs --seed 2). A test compares the browser core with it on fixed seeds, and checks k-means monotone inertia, DBSCAN against a brute-force reference, and EM's non-decreasing log-likelihood.
