      HOW IT DECIDES
      A* ROUTES
      Each ghost picks a target (Pac-Man, a cell ahead of him, or its corner) and plans a shortest route to it with A*. The glowing lines are those routes.
      Q-LEARNING
      Each move is scored as a weighted sum of hand-built features (distance to pellets, ghosts one step away, eating). Weights move after every turn toward the reward actually seen.
      SELF-PLAY
      The weights were learned by playing thousands of games against the same ghosts, with occasional random moves to explore. The rules run on the server, so every game replays exactly from its seed.

      HOW IT WORKS

      Pac-Man steps first each turn, then every ghost steps. The AI ghosts plan routes with A* search, or chance ghosts roll fixed odds. The learned agent scores each legal move as a weighted sum of 13 hand-designed features, with weights learned from 3000 self-play games.

      A* route search (the ghosts)

      New to this? A* finds a shortest path by always trying the road that looks shortest so far, with a rough guess of how far is left. The guess steers it toward the goal, so it checks far fewer routes than trying every one.

      Each ghost picks a target cell every turn and runs A* to it, with Manhattan distance (ignoring walls) as the guess. Targets by personality:

      • Chaser: Pac-Man's cell.
      • Ambusher: 4 cells ahead of Pac-Man in his last direction, stopping at a wall.
      • Scatter: Pac-Man's cell, until he is within 4 Manhattan cells; then its corner.

      Neon Lanes has three ghosts (chaser, ambusher, scatter). Vault adds a second chaser as the fourth. A scared ghost plans nothing and steps to the neighbour farthest from Pac-Man.

      Approximate Q-learning

      New to this? Rate each move with a checklist ("eats a pellet, +7", "ghost close, -20"). After each turn, compare the score you expected with what happened, and nudge the numbers to shrink the gap. Over thousands of games they settle into ratings that pick good moves.

      For each legal move the agent computes Q(s, a) = w · f(s, a): f is the 13-feature vector after that move, w the weights. It takes the highest Q, breaking exact ties at random, and in training sometimes moves at random instead (epsilon-greedy). After each turn:

      • target = r + γ · max Q(s′, ·), or just r if the game ended
      • δ = target − Q(s, a)
      • wi += α · δ · fi(s, a)

      The max uses the best move from the next position, not the one exploration picked (Watkins' off-policy form). Training reward: points this turn, minus 1, minus 300 if caught.

      Survival lookahead (one feature)

      New to this? A lookahead plays the game forward in imagination to see where a move leads, the way a chess player follows a few replies before moving.

      For each legal move, the survival feature replays the game with the real ghost logic and counts how many of the next 6 turns Pac-Man survives under his best survival play. Results are cached, so one search serves every move in a turn. It is exact for the ghost AI, except a scared ghost's random tie-break uses a fixed seed.

      Reflex rule (baseline)

      New to this? A reflex is a fixed rule of thumb, like "if the car ahead brakes, brake." It reacts to the current turn and does not plan ahead.

      Each move is scored and the highest wins: onto an active ghost, -1000; one step from an active ghost, -80; two steps, -40; onto a scared ghost, +50; otherwise minus the walking distance to the nearest pellet. These values were hand-written, not tuned.

      Random (floor)

      New to this? It rolls a die at each junction. It sets the floor any real strategy should beat.

      Picks uniformly among legal moves.

      Chance ghosts (fixed odds)

      New to this? Like rolling a die at each junction. The ghost does not look at the maze or at Pac-Man; it only rolls the table.

      Each turn a chance ghost picks a direction from fixed odds, read relative to the way it last moved. The shares are renormalised over the directions that are open, so a ghost in a corridor with one way out takes it.

      No search and no lookahead. The shares were set by hand, not fitted to data. Choose it from the GHOSTS menu, or run python -m pacman match to play each agent against both ghost policies on the same seeds.

      How these were chosen: The features and their scales (ranges of 6 and 8 cells, distance divided by 10, openness by 4, survival by 6) were designed by hand; distance is linear so far targets keep a gradient. The weights were not set by hand: training started from zeros and learned all 13. The reward constants (1 per turn, 300 for being caught) are hand-set, and the point values come from the game rules. No tuning sweep over any of these is committed. Features overlap, so read weights together: survival's 319.34 is about 53 per extra surviving turn (319.34 / 6).

      Training

      Self-play alternates between Neon Lanes and Vault for 3000 games, trainer seed 1, starting from zero weights. Step size α falls linearly from 0.01 to 0.001 over the run. Discount γ is 0.9. Exploration ε falls linearly from 0.3 to 0.02 over the first 2400 games, then stays at 0.02. Games stop at 300 turns. These are the trainer's defaults, and the trained block in weights.json records the same settings. The last 25 training games, at ε = 0.02, averaged 852 points and 5 wins. Those are training games, not the benchmark.
